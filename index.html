The dish is stocked once, at the start, and after that nothing steps in: if every lineage dies out, it stays dead until you release something new from here. There's no body-plan editor — every functional part traces back to a real folded protein (see the Species tab), so there's nothing to hand-pick. This releases a population from a fresh, fully random gene sequence, guaranteed a viable way to feed itself. Its brain starts random — evolution shapes how it actually behaves over the generations that follow.

Reproduction

Asexual: reproduces solo, child is a mutated clone. Sexual: two ready adults of this species have to physically meet — their offspring's traits and brain are shuffled from both parents, then mutated. Harder to pull off, more genetic mixing.

Sexual reproducers in particular need a decent founding population — a mate has to actually be nearby and ready too, so a handful of individuals can struggle against an established rival for the same food.

One card per species currently alive in the dish — whether you released it, or it split off from another lineage once its genes drifted far enough to earn its own name. Tap a card for its full stat star.

The primordial pool, live — free amino acids and nucleotides drift and occasionally bond when energy happens to be nearby. Folded peptides can become catalysts; RNA long and structured enough can start templating copies of itself, mutations and all. Lipids self-assemble into membranes for free, and a membrane that closes around a working replicator becomes a protocell. The moment one proves itself (an active catalyst, real replication, survived a division) it swims out into the dish on its own as a new founding lineage — see it happen live, right where it bootstrapped. Once the dish has a real, sustained population of its own, the pool retires — a fresh abiogenesis event has effectively no chance of competing against an ecosystem already dozens of generations deep, so that compute goes back to the living population instead.

Pool retired — the dish sustained its own population long enough that the primordial soup stopped simulating. The stats below are frozen at the moment it stopped.

Manual seeding

Place specific chemistry directly into the pool by hand — a way to hand a stalled vesicle the one thing it's missing rather than wait on the vent's random trickle. This is a deliberate override, same idea as releasing a random population in the Designer tab, not part of the pool's own emergent economy.
